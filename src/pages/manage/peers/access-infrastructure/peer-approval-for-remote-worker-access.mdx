# Peer Approval for Remote Worker Access with openZro

For organizations embracing remote work, ensuring secure network access for distributed teams is a paramount challenge. Traditionally, VPNs and remote desktop solutions have been the standard for granting access to company resources. However, these methods often fall short in today's dynamic work environment, especially when dealing with freelancers and temporary workers.

The conventional approach to remote worker access presents several security and operational challenges:

* **Increased Security Risks**: Granting blanket access to network resources can expose sensitive data to potential breaches, especially when dealing with external collaborators.
* **Device Management Complexity**: Ensuring that only authorized and secure devices connect to the network becomes increasingly difficult as the number of remote workers grows.
* **Lack of Granular Control**: Traditional solutions often lack the flexibility to implement fine-grained access policies based on user roles and device trust levels.
* **Scalability Issues**: As teams expand and contract, managing access for a fluctuating workforce can become a time-consuming and error-prone process.

This guide introduces openZro's Peer Approval as a robust solution for secure remote worker access by:

* **Implementing Zero-Trust Principles**: Ensuring that every device and user is verified before granting network access, regardless of their location.
* **Simplifying Device Trust Management**: Providing a streamlined process for approving and managing trusted devices within the network.
* **Enhancing Access Control**: Offering granular control over network resources, allowing organizations to tailor access based on user roles and device status.
* **Improving Scalability**: Facilitating easy onboarding and offboarding of remote workers, including freelancers, without compromising network security.

Let's explore the step-by-step process of implementing [Peer Approval with openZro](/manage/peers/approve-peers) to ensure that only trusted devices can access your network.

## Prerequisites

To replicate this use case, you'll need the following prerequisites:

* A self-hosted openZro management server (see the [self-hosting guide](/selfhosted/selfhosted-quickstart)) and an
  administrator account.
* A way to give the freelancer an account: an [identity provider](/selfhosted/identity-providers) they can sign in
  with, or [local users](/manage/team/idp-sync/scim-local-users) enabled on your deployment.
* [openZro installed](/get-started/install) on the main device.

With these prerequisites in place, you're ready to simulate granting network access to a temporary remote worker using openZro's Peer Approval feature by:

1. Setting up openZro's access control policies for enhanced security
2. Enabling peer approval
3. Giving the freelancer an account
4. Installing openZro on the remote worker device
5. Approving peers
6. Combining approval with Device Admission (optional)

## 1. Setting Up openZro's Access Control Policies For Enhanced Security

Before onboarding remote workers, ensure your organization has appropriate [access control policies](/manage/access-control/manage-network-access) in place. Adhering to zero-trust principles, create or modify policies to grant new users access only to necessary resources.

Go to **Access Control** in the dashboard, then click **Add Policy** or edit an existing one. Here's a sample policy
that grants members of the `Freelancers` group access to PostgreSQL (TCP 5432) on the peers in the `On-Premise-DB`
group: **Source** `Freelancers`, **Destination** `On-Premise-DB`, **Protocol** TCP, **Ports** 5432.

![openZro Freelancer Access Control Policy](/docs-static/img/manage/peers/access-infrastructure/peer-approval-for-remote-worker-access/peer-0-01.png)

If necessary, you can also set [posture checks](/manage/access-control/posture-checks) for this policy.

![openZro Freelancer Posture Check](/docs-static/img/manage/peers/access-infrastructure/peer-approval-for-remote-worker-access/peer-0-02.png)

Moreover, it is a best practice to disable the `Default` policy to enforce only restrictive, custom-defined access controls.

![openZro Access Policy View](/docs-static/img/manage/peers/access-infrastructure/peer-approval-for-remote-worker-access/peer-0-03.png)

With appropriate access policies in place, you're ready to enable openZro's Peer Approval feature.

## 2. Enabling Peer Approval

To enable peer approval, go to **Settings → Authentication**, turn on **Require peer approval** in the
**Peer approval** card, and click **Save Changes**. To keep approval for third parties only, list your internal groups
under **Groups that skip approval**.

![Peer approval settings](/docs-static/img/manage/peers/approve-peers/peer-approval-settings.png)

With peer approval on, a new device connects to the management server but gets no network access until an
administrator approves it.

## 3. Giving the Freelancer an Account

openZro does not send invitation emails. Give the freelancer an account in one of two ways:

* **Local user.** If [local users](/manage/team/idp-sync/scim-local-users) are enabled, go to **Users & Groups**, click
  **Create local user**, and enter the freelancer's email and an initial password. Under **Auto-assigned groups**,
  select `Freelancers`. Share the credentials with the freelancer over a secure channel.
* **Identity provider.** If the freelancer signs in through your identity provider, they appear in **Users & Groups**
  after their first login. Open the user and add `Freelancers` to their **Auto-assigned groups**.

Auto-assigned groups are applied to every peer the user adds, so the freelancer's device lands in `Freelancers` and
gets the policy from step 1.

## 4. Installing openZro on the Remote Worker Device

When the freelancer signs in to the dashboard, they see **Add a new device to your network** with install
instructions for their operating system, because regular users cannot see the peer list by default. They
[install openZro](/get-started/install) and connect:

```bash
openzro up --management-url https://<YOUR_MANAGEMENT_DOMAIN>
```

The client opens the browser to sign in. After that, the device is registered and waits for approval.

## 5. Approving Peers

Back in your administrator account, the freelancer's device shows **Approval pending** in the **Peers** list. Use the
**Pending** filter to find it. To grant network access:

* Open the device's row menu (**⋮**) and choose **Approve**, or open the device and click **Approve** next to its name
* Confirm the action when prompted

![Approve in the row menu of a pending peer](/docs-static/img/manage/peers/approve-peers/peer-needs-approval.png)

After approval, the device can reach the resources allowed for the `Freelancers` group. To check what it can reach,
open the device and select the **Accessible Peers** tab.

![openZro Main Account Peers View](/docs-static/img/manage/peers/access-infrastructure/peer-approval-for-remote-worker-access/peer-a-17.png)

## 6. Combining Approval with Device Admission (optional)

Peer approval relies on an administrator's judgment. To also require that the device is managed and compliant,
connect your MDM or EDR provider (Microsoft Intune, SentinelOne, Huntress or CrowdStrike Falcon) under
**Integrations → MDM / EDR**, and enable [Device Admission](/manage/access-control/posture-checks/device-admission).
Device Admission refuses devices that the provider does not report as compliant at enrollment, login and sync, so a
freelancer's unmanaged laptop is turned away before it reaches the approval queue. Device Admission does not approve
peers on its own: both gates apply.

For provider setup, see [EDR integrations](/manage/access-control/endpoint-detection-and-response).
