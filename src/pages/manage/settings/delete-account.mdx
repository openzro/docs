# Delete the account record

In openZro, the **account record** is the per-tenant container in the
management server's database that owns your peers, users, groups,
policies, network resources, and routes. Deleting it through the
dashboard removes that record and all of its associated data — but
**it does not uninstall openZro itself**. The management server keeps
running and is ready to create a fresh account on the next IdP login.

To delete the account, you must be signed in as a user with the
[owner role](/manage/team/add-users-to-your-network#manage-user-roles).
If you aren't the owner, ask the owner to do it.

<Note>
    This action is irreversible. Peers, users (including service users), setup keys, groups, policies, routes,
    nameservers and DNS zones, networks and their resources, and posture checks are permanently deleted. There is no
    recovery from the dashboard; you'd have to restore the management server's database from a backup.
</Note>

What is **not** removed:

- the activity log, which keeps its history and records the deletion itself;
- configuration stored outside the account record, such as activity streamers, flow-event exports and MDM/EDR
  providers.

<Note>
    If the account has local users provisioned through SCIM, deletion is refused. Delete those users through SCIM
    first.
</Note>

## Steps

1. Open **Settings** → **Danger Zone**. Only the owner sees this tab.
2. Review the warning and click **Delete Account**.

   <p>
       <img src="/docs-static/img/manage/settings/account-settings-danger-zone.png" alt="Delete openZro account in the Danger Zone" className="imagewrapper-big"/>
   </p>
3. Confirm by clicking **Delete**.

   <p>
       <img src="/docs-static/img/manage/settings/account-settings-delete-account-confirm.png" alt="Delete account confirmation" className="imagewrapper"/>
   </p>

After deletion, your session ends and you're redirected to the login
page. If you log in again, a fresh account record is created
automatically.

## What about the management server itself?

To uninstall the management binary and tear down the deployment
entirely (database, configuration, certificates), see
[Remove openZro](/selfhosted/maintenance/remove) under self-host
maintenance.
