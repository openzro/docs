# Managing Access with openZro: Groups and Access Policies

openZro lets administrators control access between peers using groups and access policies. A policy defines which
peers or groups can connect, which protocols and ports they can use, and optionally which posture checks they must
pass. Posture checks make access depend on the state of the device, in line with zero-trust principles.

<Note>
    For a visual overview of your access policies and network topology, see the [Control Center](/manage/control-center).
</Note>

## Introduction
When the first user logs in to a new openZro deployment, management creates a `Default` policy that lets all peers
connect over any protocol, forming a full mesh. This suits small networks. When you need to restrict access, create
policies with narrower groups and disable or delete the `Default` policy. Self-hosted deployments can skip the
`Default` policy entirely by setting `DisableDefaultPolicy: true` in `management.json`.

Policies use groups to control connections between peers. Groups are sets of peers and can be used as the source or
destination of a policy. Management evaluates policies when it distributes the network map to each peer.

## Concepts
### Groups
An openZro group works like a tag in other platforms: you create it, assign it to peers, and use it in policies.

- A peer can be in several groups, and a group can have many peers.
- Groups can be used as the source and destination of policies.
- Groups are created on the **Groups** page or inline, wherever a group selector is shown. [Learn more](#creating-groups)
- The default `All` group cannot be renamed or deleted.

<Note>
    You can assign groups automatically with the [peer auto-grouping feature](/manage/peers/register-machines-using-setup-keys#peer-auto-grouping).
</Note>

### The All Group
The `All` group automatically includes every peer in your network. It cannot be modified or removed.

### Policies
Policies define how peers can communicate: who can initiate connections (the source), who they can reach (the
destination), and over which protocols and ports.

Policies only allow traffic; there are no deny rules, so there is no processing order or priority. Without any policy,
peers cannot communicate with each other.

Each policy is either **bidirectional**, letting both sides initiate connections, or **one-way**, letting only the
source initiate connections to the destination. Direction applies to every protocol, including ALL and ICMP. Replies
to connections the source opened are always allowed.

<Note>
    A one-way policy with protocol ALL is marked experimental in the editor. Reply traffic relies on the firewall's
    connection tracking, and traffic the destination starts on its own (SNMP traps, syslog push, unsolicited ICMP) is
    dropped.
</Note>

<Note>
    To let peers in the same group communicate with each other, add the same group as both source and destination.
</Note>

### The Default policy
The `Default` policy uses the [`All`](#the-all-group) group as both source and destination, so every peer can reach
every other peer over any protocol. Because policies only allow traffic, restrictive policies have no visible effect
while `Default` is active. Disable or delete it once your own policies are in place.

## Managing Policies

### Creating Policies
Go to **Access Control** and click **Add Policy**. The policy editor opens:

1. Enter a **Policy name** and, optionally, a **Description**. **Status** sets whether the policy is active.
2. On the **Policy** tab, choose the **Source · From** and **Destination · To** groups. You can select existing groups
   or type a name to create one. Use the direction toggle between them to make the policy bidirectional or one-way.
3. On the **Ports & Protocol** tab, choose the protocol: ALL, TCP, UDP or ICMP. For TCP and UDP you can list ports and
   port ranges, for example `22, 443, 8000-9000`. Leave the ports empty to allow all ports.
4. Optionally, on the **Posture Checks** tab, attach [posture checks](/manage/access-control/posture-checks) that the
   source peers must pass.
5. Click **Add policy**.

The **Live preview** panel next to the form summarizes the policy as you edit it, and **Impact** shows how many peers
it affects and reaches.

<Note>
    We recommend using [identity provider (IdP) integrations](/manage/team/idp-sync) to provision your user groups from the IdP.
</Note>

### Adding peers to groups
If you create a new group when defining a policy, add peers to it for the policy to take effect. Go to **Peers**,
open the peer, and select the group(s) under **Assigned Groups**.

<p>
    <img src="/docs-static/img/manage/access-control/associate-peer-groups.png" alt="Assigned Groups on the peer page" className="imagewrapper-big"/>
</p>

<Note>
    You can assign groups automatically with the [peer auto-grouping feature](/manage/peers/register-machines-using-setup-keys#peer-auto-grouping).
</Note>

### Updating Policies

To update a policy, click its name in the **Access Control** list. The same editor opens; change what you need and
click **Save changes**.

### Disabling Policies
To disable a policy, use the switch in the **Active** column of the list.

### Deleting Policies
To delete a policy, open its row menu (**⋮**), choose **Delete** and confirm.

## Managing Groups

### Creating Groups

You can create groups in two ways:

**Inline**<br/>
Wherever a group selector is shown, for example in the policy editor, type the new group's name and press Enter.

**From the Groups page**<br/>
1. Go to **Users & Groups** and open the **Groups** tab.
2. Click **New Group**.
3. Enter a name and click **Create Group**.

### Viewing Groups

The **Groups** tab lists all groups with the objects that use them (peers, users, policies and more). Filter by
**All**, **Used** or **Unused** to find groups you can clean up.

<p>
    <img src="/docs-static/img/manage/access-control/groups/view-groups.png" alt="Groups list" className="imagewrapper-big"/>
</p>

### Renaming Groups

1. On the **Groups** tab, open the row menu (**⋮**) of the group.
2. Choose **Edit**, enter the new name and click **Save Changes**.

<Note>
Groups synchronized from Identity Providers (Google Workspace, Entra ID, etc.) cannot be renamed.
</Note>

### Deleting Groups

1. On the **Groups** tab, open the row menu (**⋮**) of the group.
2. Choose **Delete** and confirm.

<Note>
Groups synchronized from Identity Providers (Google Workspace, Entra ID, etc.) cannot be deleted.
</Note>

<Note>
Groups that are still in use (by peers, policies, routes, setup keys and so on) cannot be deleted. Remove those
dependencies first.
</Note>
